Victims of the canopy collapse: “They will live on through our stories”

Novi Sad, 1 November 2024, 11:52. The canopy of the city's newly renovated railway station collapsed, killing fourteen people. Two more later died of their injuries. A year on, their families and friends told BBC News Serbian about the people they loved, and how they want them to be remembered.

Digital Storytelling and Social Media Award, VOICES 2026, Thessaloniki

What happened

On 1 November 2024, at 11:52, the concrete canopy of the newly renovated railway station in Novi Sad, Serbia's second-largest city, collapsed onto people waiting below. Fourteen were killed at the scene and two more died later in hospital. One woman was seriously injured.

Many in Serbia blamed corruption and negligence in the renovation. Daily silences at 11:52 grew into the country's largest protest movement in decades, led by students who blockaded almost every university. Under public pressure, the construction minister and later the prime minister resigned. Criminal proceedings are under way against several former officials, including the former construction minister, but no one has been convicted.

In October 2026, a list backed by the student movement won an outright majority in snap parliamentary elections, ending the rule of the Serbian Progressive Party, in power since 2012. On the second anniversary, thousands stood in silence outside the station for 16 minutes, one for each victim.

“He fell in love with Novi Sad. He told me: ‘I'm never going back to Belgrade, I'm staying in Novi Sad.’ And he really did stay in Novi Sad.”

Dijana Hrka, about her son Stefan Hrka, who was killed

Oni koji su otišli i oni koji ostaju (Those Who Left and Those Who Remain) was published by BBC News Serbian for the first anniversary of the collapse. Over several weeks, our journalists met the families and friends of the victims across Serbia. They shared photographs, voice messages and letters.

“Miloš adored Anđela, and she adored him. Whatever she wanted, Miloš would make happen. If she'd said, ‘I want to go to Mars,’ I think he would have found her a way to get there.”

Zorica Ruman, about her daughter Anđela and Anđela's boyfriend Miloš Milosavljević, who were both killed
